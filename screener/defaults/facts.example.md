# Facts

Replace everything in this file with your own background. Screening refuses
to run until you do. It stays on your machine, apart from being sent with each
posting to the model that scores it.

This file is the only thing the screener may treat as true about you, and it is
what cover letter drafts will be allowed to claim. If a posting asks for
something that is not in here, that is a gap: the screener should score it as
one and a draft should either skip it or name it plainly.

The point of the format is the guardrails, not the headings. Write down what you
did, then write down the way it is most likely to get inflated, because a tool
that rounds your background up produces scores you cannot trust and cover
letters you cannot send. Be specific about scale, and about the difference
between leading a thing and helping with it.

## Current role

Job title and employer. What you are actually responsible for, and how big the
environment really is.

- State the scope in numbers, and never let it drift upward.
- If someone else holds the senior role, say so here.

## Projects that are yours

The work you personally did and could be questioned on in an interview.

- Say which parts you owned outright.
- Say which parts were partial, and in what proportion.
- Write the overclaim you want blocked, in the form "never say X."

## Projects you contributed to

Work you helped with but did not own or lead. Keep this separate from the
section above; the difference is the first thing an interviewer probes.

- List only your own tasks, not the project's goals.

## Stack

The tools you have genuinely used. Note where you deployed or operated something
rather than designed it, since that distinction decides a lot of postings.

## Earlier work

Previous roles or freelance work, with dates, and an honest note on what the
experience does and does not transfer to.

## Credentials

Certifications you hold. Education, including its absence. Both matter to the
screener because postings state them as hard requirements.

## What this is not

The overclaims to block, said plainly. The screener is told to hold to this
section rather than round your background up, so it is the most useful part of
the file. For example:

- One IT role, in a 40-person office. Nothing at enterprise scale.
- No degree. No security clearance.
- Contributed to the tenant migration. Did not lead it or design it.
- Configured the firewall from a vendor template. Cannot design a network.
